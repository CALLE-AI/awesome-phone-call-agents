# VouchCall Demo Call Scripts

You will call yourself 3 times, playing a different reference each time.
The candidate is **Alex Morgan**, applying for **Senior Software Engineer**.

Wait for VouchCall to ask each question, then answer naturally using these guides.
Don't read word-for-word — just use these as talking points. Sound natural.

---

## Call 1: Jordan Lee (Former Manager) — GLOWING REVIEW

**Your character:** You were Alex's direct manager for 2 years at a mid-size tech company.

When asked about your relationship:
> "I managed Alex directly for about two years. They were a senior dev on my team working on our backend services."

When asked about strengths:
> "Honestly, Alex is one of the best engineers I've managed. They have this ability to just take ownership of hard problems. During our service migration last year, Alex pretty much led the whole thing and delivered it early. And they're great with the team — always helping unblock other engineers."

When asked about teamwork:
> "Fantastic. Alex is the kind of person who makes the whole team better. They do code reviews thoroughly, they mentor junior devs, and they're always the first to offer help."

When asked about reliability/deadlines:
> "Very reliable. I could give Alex a project and know it would get done. They're good at breaking things down and communicating progress. I never had to chase them."

When asked about areas for growth:
> "If I had to pick one thing... Alex could be a bit more vocal in larger meetings. In one-on-ones and small groups they're great, but in all-hands or cross-team meetings they tend to stay quiet. For a senior role that's worth working on."

When asked to rate 1-10:
> "I'd say a 9. I'd hire them again in a heartbeat."

## Call 2: Priya Sharma (Team Lead / Peer) — POSITIVE BUT FLAGS CONCERNS

**Your character:** You were a team lead on a parallel team. Worked alongside Alex for about 18 months on shared projects.

When asked about your relationship:
> "We were on adjacent teams — I led the platform team and Alex was on the services team. We collaborated on a few cross-team projects over about a year and a half."

When asked about strengths:
> "Technically, Alex is outstanding. Probably one of the strongest engineers I've worked with. They have a really creative approach to architecture — they'll find elegant solutions that others wouldn't think of."

When asked about teamwork:
> "Within their own team, great. On cross-team stuff it was... mostly good. Sometimes Alex would go deep on a solution without checking in, and we'd realize the direction had drifted from what we agreed on."

When asked about reliability/deadlines:
> "This is where I'd be honest — there were a couple of times where deadlines slipped. Not on small stuff, but on bigger cross-team deliverables. I think Alex tends to perfect things rather than ship them. When focused on one thing the output is outstanding, but when juggling multiple projects, things could fall through the cracks."

When asked about areas for growth:
> "Definitely the deadline thing. Also, I think Alex can over-engineer sometimes. Not everything needs to be the most elegant solution — sometimes good enough on time is better than perfect and late."

When asked to rate 1-10:
> "I'd say a 7. Good engineer, I'd recommend them, but whoever manages them should be aware of the multitasking thing."

## Call 3: Michael Chen (Skip-Level Manager) — CAUTIOUS / MIXED

**Your character:** You were a senior director — Alex's manager's manager. You observed Alex from a higher level for about a year.

When asked about your relationship:
> "I was the senior director overseeing Alex's team. So I didn't work with them day-to-day, but I observed their work in project reviews and cross-functional meetings for about a year."

When asked about strengths:
> "Alex does solid technical work, no question. When they take on a deep technical problem, the output is consistently good. They're a strong individual contributor."

When asked about teamwork:
> "At the team level, fine. At the cross-functional level... that's where I'd want to see growth. In meetings with product and design, Alex wasn't always the best at translating technical decisions into business context."

When asked about reliability/deadlines:
> "For the technical work itself, decent. My concern was more about visibility — there were times I had to chase Alex for status updates on critical projects. At a senior level, I expect engineers to proactively communicate up, not wait to be asked."

When asked about areas for growth:
> "Two things. One, communication with non-technical stakeholders. Two, leadership presence. For a senior engineer role, I'd want to see more initiative in driving direction, not just executing well. Alex is great at the 'what' but could improve on the 'why' and 'so what' for the broader organization."

When asked to rate 1-10:
> "I'd say a 6. Good engineer, but for a senior role specifically, I'd want to see more leadership maturity. If this were a mid-level role, I'd be more enthusiastic."

## Tips for Recording

1. **Use a different tone for each character** — Jordan is warm and enthusiastic, Priya is thoughtful and measured, Michael is professional and slightly reserved.
2. **Pause before answering** — makes it sound like you're thinking, not reading.
3. **Add filler words naturally** — "um", "you know", "honestly" make it sound real.
4. **Don't rush** — let each call breathe. About 2-3 minutes per call.
5. **You can deviate from the script** — these are just guides. The AI will adapt.
